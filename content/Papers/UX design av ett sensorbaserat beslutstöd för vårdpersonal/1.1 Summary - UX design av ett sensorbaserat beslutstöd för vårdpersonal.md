**Subject**: UX in healthcare

**Summary**:

>The article presents the design of a sensor-based decision support system to help nursing staff in elderly care. The goal was to improve efficiency and care quality by collecting health data from sensors and presenting it in an easy-to-understand way for caregivers. 

**Conclusions**:

- Manually analyzing large amounts of health data is is too time-consuming.
- Healthcare workers are willing to use AI but prefer to make their own final. decisions rather than to rely on AI completely.
- Healthcare staff report that limited time and high staff turnover create a need for decision support systems that summarize health data and help identify important changes.

**Other facts**:

- About one-third of adults over the age of 65 experience at least one fall each year.
- Among older adults who fall, approximately 10–20% experience serious outcomes, such as hospitalization, injury, or premature death.
- More than 60% of people over 80 years old have abnormal gait values, making gait an important indicator for monitoring mobility and fall risk.
- Monitoring sleep position is clinically relevant because older adults at risk of pressure ulcers require regular repositioning to prevent skin damage (bed sores).
- People are living longer today, which means there are more older adults in society. This increases the need for elderly care and raises healthcare costs. Therefore, it is valuable to explore how welfare technology can be used to support and improve care for older people.
- Examples of health data (hälsodata in Swedish) include sleep, balance, number of toilet visits, blood pressure, and heart rate etc.
- AI can improve elderly care by supporting personalized care, detecting health issues early, increasing independence, and providing social support. However, AI solutions for elderly care are still in the early stages and are not yet widely used in care homes.
