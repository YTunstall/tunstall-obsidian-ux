**Subject**: UX in healthcare

**Summary**:

>The article presents the design of a sensor-based decision support system to help nursing staff in elderly care. The goal was to improve efficiency and care quality by collecting health data from sensors and presenting it in an easy-to-understand way for caregivers. 

**Conclusions**:

- Manually analyzing large amounts of health data is is too time-consuming.
- Healthcare workers are willing to use AI but prefer to make their own final. decisions rather than to rely on AI completely.
- Healthcare staff report that limited time and high staff turnover create a need for decision support systems that summarize health data and help identify important changes.
- Displaying too much health data at once can make the system difficult to use. Without AI, caregivers must spend time analyzing and connecting different health indicators, which can be challenging in a time-constrained environment.
- By providing an analysis of residents' health status, the system can support caregivers in prioritizing tasks and allocating their time more effectively. The analysis can also be particularly helpful for new staff or caregivers who are less familiar with the residents.

**Relevant facts**:

- Examples of health data (hälsodata in Swedish) include sleep, balance, number of toilet visits, blood pressure, and heart rate etc.
- AI can improve elderly care by supporting personalized care, detecting health issues early, increasing independence, and providing social support. However, AI solutions for elderly care are still in the early stages and are not yet widely used in care homes.
- AI can analyze health data and provide recommendations for appropriate actions. This can save time for caregivers by reducing the need for manual data analysis, allowing them to focus more on direct patient care.
- An AI-generated summary can be valuable and well received by caregivers, but human judgment should remain part of the decision-making process. To improve transparency, the interface could include a small icon or label indicating that the summary was generated by AI. Since AI-generated analyses may occasionally be inaccurate, caregivers should be aware that the system is not infallible and should use its insights as decision support rather than relying on them exclusively.
- Incorrect AI suggestions can reduce user trust. Allowing caregivers to reject suggestions and provide feedback could help improve the system over time, reducing errors and increasing confidence in its recommendations.
- A wide range of health data is valuable because older adults often have complex and varying healthcare needs. While large amounts of data can make it difficult for caregivers to get a quick overview, AI can analyze the data, identify patterns, and generate insights. Integrating additional sources, such as medical records and care documentation systems, could further improve analysis and reduce caregivers' workload.
- During handovers between caregivers, information should be kept concise and include clear insights to support quick understanding. Data older than 24 hours is usually less relevant and does not need to be shown by default, but it should still be available if needed.
- Health data should be collected as comprehensively as possible, as older adults often have complex and diverse healthcare needs. Integrating data from medical records and other care systems could provide a more complete overview and reduce the need for caregivers to combine information from multiple sources.
- A good fix for different opinions on an interface is to further simplify it and add personalizing features (pliability/formbarhet).
- For a welfare technology application to be effective, the system should be tailored to different professional roles, as each group requires different types of health data and information.
- If possible when presenting two sets of data, only display changes rather than unchanged aspects.

**Other facts**:

- About one-third of adults over the age of 65 experience at least one fall each year.
- Among older adults who fall, approximately 10–20% experience serious outcomes, such as hospitalization, injury, or premature death.
- More than 60% of people over 80 years old have abnormal gait values, making gait an important indicator for monitoring mobility and fall risk.
- Monitoring sleep position is clinically relevant because older adults at risk of pressure ulcers require regular repositioning to prevent skin damage (bed sores).
- People are living longer today, which means there are more older adults in society. This increases the need for elderly care and raises healthcare costs. Therefore, it is valuable to explore how welfare technology can be used to support and improve care for older people.

**Future possibilities**
- Future work could include designing interfaces for additional professional roles, such as nurses, physiotherapists, and occupational therapists, as their information needs may differ from those of caregivers. The system could also be explored for managers and family members, who may benefit from tailored views and access to relevant health information
