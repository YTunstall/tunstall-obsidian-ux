Here you can find a collection of important general design principles to guide you through the design process and support informed decision-making. Understanding these principles is fundamental to create user-centered design.

**Aesthetics** - Makes interfaces attractive *without* sacrificing usability

>*Aesthetics* are highly subjective, but certain visual principles such as symmetry tend to have a positive effect on most users. This is because symmetry is a natural phenomenon that occurs frequently in nature as a result of biological growth, physical laws, and evolutionary processes. As a result, people often perceive symmetrical designs as more balanced, harmonious, and visually appealing.

**Empathy** - Understanding users before designing
  
>*Empathy* enables us to create products that are not only functional but also meaningful, accessible, and enjoyable for the people who use them. By grounding design decisions in real, tangible user data, we can make more informed and accurate choices that positively impact the overall user experience.  

 **Ethics** - Respect users' privacy, autonomy, and accessibility
 
>*Ethics* plays a fundamental role in design by ensuring that products are created responsibly and with the user's best interests in mind. Ethical design means respecting users' privacy, promoting accessibility, ensuring fairness, and being transparent about how products work and how data is collected and used. It also involves avoiding deceptive practices, such as dark patterns, that manipulate users into making decisions they did not intend to make
 
 **Evidence** (Epistemology) - Testing ideas instead of guessing
   
>*Evidence* is essential for making informed design decisions. We naturally make assumptions based on our own experiences, but these assumptions can lead to poor design decisions because they may not reflect the needs of real users. Rather than relying on guesswork, designers should validate their ideas through evidence such as user research, usability testing, analytics, and feedback.

**Pragmatism** - Iterate based on real-world feedback

>*Pragmatism* emphasizes designing solutions that work effectively in the real world rather than relying solely on assumptions or theory. In UX design, this means continuously testing ideas, gathering real user feedback, and iterating based on how people actually interact with a product. 

 **Simplicity** - Reduce unnecessary complexity
 
>Simplicity is a fundamental design principle that focuses on removing unnecessary complexity and presenting information in a clear and intuitive way. A simple design helps users understand and interact with a product more easily, reducing cognitive load and minimizing the risk of errors. By prioritizing essential features and eliminating distractions (noise).
